Let's get serious, though. When I came into this project, I had no plan or idea for the layout of my website. Most of it was simply created by feeling and intuition, evolving from my selection of bright colors and simple shapes. My design plans now have a seed to sprout from, and that has in turn been the gift of glorious CSS formatting.

I feel that my color palette choices and subtle typefaces compliment how I want to present myself best. Green and simple have been the two best words to describe how I want to present myself for the last few years, thanks to some radical shifts in how I identify myself and display online. Plus, I much prefer the authenticity of a color scheme I like plus a font that doesn't scream in your face. It feels more like I'm the one who's speaking to you.

Semantic HTML and a contrast score checker on hand makes things simple for accessibility. The only issue I've run into for the test page was the dark green color I used, which is a specifically pleasing shade of green to me, but loses triple-A rating unless used exclusively with icons and large text. The primary accessibility decision I had to make was selecting text colors that would contrast well with the page theming. Depending on how people interact with the pages I make, knowing I belong to a demographic with plenty of other folks with disabilities has made me want to make it as readable and exact as possible.

Creating one of these systems was... much more difficult than it seemed. Font choices were pretty important, as was finding contrast for my green hues. It's hard to find stylistic elements, especially when you're just starting out and have nothing to go off of except the vibes in your heart. I plan to apply this system to my portfolio project by using the same color palette and a similar design scheme for interactive elements. It is difficult to visualize the final layout without having it in front of me, sure, but it does help to have a color scheme on hand.

The palette limit seems a bit restrictive for future expansion, so I'm planning to add some pink in for the future for links and other specific elements as an accent. Font choice is pretty simplistic, and therefore flexible, as is keeping the colors solid and minimizing effects. I want the page to be pretty, but content comes first, and applying the KISS principle will serve me very well in both dev time and page utility.
